# Choosing healthy carbs — CDC

Carbs can be part of a healthy diet for people with diabetes; the key is portion and choice.

- Simple carbs raise blood sugar quickly: table sugar, honey, fruit juice, syrup, and foods with added sugar such as soda, cookies, cakes and candies. Some simple carbs come in healthy foods like whole fruit and dairy.
- Refined grains have the fibre removed: white bread, white rice, white pasta, many cereals and pastries.
- Complex carbs raise blood sugar more slowly because of fibre: starchy vegetables, legumes (beans, lentils) and whole grains.

One serving of carbs is 15 grams. When eating carbs, add a protein source (meat, a small handful of nuts, low-fat dairy) to stay fuller and avoid spikes.

Healthy swaps: eat whole fruit instead of juice (about 3 oranges make 1 cup of juice); rethink sugar-sweetened drinks (soda, sweetened tea and coffee) and choose water; choose whole-wheat bread and pasta; try oats instead of sugary cereals; try other grains such as millet, barley, bulgur.
