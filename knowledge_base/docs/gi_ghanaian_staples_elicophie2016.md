# Glycaemic index (GI) of Ghanaian staples — Eli-Cophie et al.

GI classes used by the study: low ≤55, medium 56–69, high ≥70. GI compares the blood-glucose rise after a food containing 50 g available carbohydrate with the rise after 50 g pure glucose (GI = 100).

Study design: crossover trial in 10 healthy, non-diabetic adults (8 men, 2 women, aged 20–50) at KNUST, Kumasi; capillary glucose measured over 2 hours; methods per FAO/WHO 1998. Test foods were served with about 110 g of the same light soup and a serving of salmon. Banku was made from corn dough and cassava dough (80:20); tuo zaafi (TZ) from unfermented maize flour; Ga kenkey from fermented corn dough; processed fufu from an industrial flour containing plantain, cassava and potato.

| Food (as tested) | GI | SE | GI class | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Banku (corn:cassava dough 80:20) | 73 | 5.0 | High | Eli-Cophie 2016, Table 2 |
| Tuo zaafi (TZ) | 68 | 7.5 | Medium | Eli-Cophie 2016, Table 2 |
| Fufu, locally pounded | 55 | 8.7 | Low | Eli-Cophie 2016, Table 2 |
| Ga kenkey | 41 | 6.8 | Low | Eli-Cophie 2016, Table 2 |
| Fufu, from industrially processed flour | 31 | 6.6 | Low | Eli-Cophie 2016, Table 2 |

Findings: GI of banku differed significantly from kenkey (P < 0.05) although both are corn-based; pounded fufu differed significantly from processed-flour fufu (p = 0.026). Even though both fufus were low GI, the glucose response area for locally pounded fufu was about 33% higher. The authors conclude Ghanaian staples range from low to moderately high GI and this should inform dietary recommendations for people with diabetes.

Caveats for OnTrack answers:
- Only 10 healthy people without diabetes; responses in people with diabetes may differ.
- GI applies to the tested recipe and a 50 g available-carbohydrate portion. Portion size (glycaemic load), the soup, fish and vegetables eaten with it, and preparation change the real effect.
- A low GI does not make a food "free" — large balls of kenkey or fufu still contain a lot of carbohydrate.
- Use the class with the number: locally pounded fufu GI 55 is low; banku GI 73 is high. Do not call banku a lighter or lower-GI swap for fufu.
- If a cited GI range spans more than one class (low ≤55, medium 56–69, high ≥70), do not call that food low.
