# GI of Ghanaian yam varieties by cooking method — Ampofo et al. 2021

Design: 10 healthy subjects aged 20–50; capillary glucose over 2 hours. Yams were boiled (35–45 min), deep-fried in vegetable oil (35–40 min) or roasted unpeeled (about 40–45 min).

| Yam and cooking method | GI (mean ± SD) | Class | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| White yam (D. rotundata), boiled | 44.26 ± 12.67 | Low | Ampofo 2021, Table 1 |
| Water yam (D. alata), boiled | 50.12 ± 18.91 | Low | Ampofo 2021, Table 1 |
| White yam, roasted | 50.62 ± 19.81 | Low | Ampofo 2021, Table 1 |
| Water yam, roasted | 54.04 ± 13.25 | Low | Ampofo 2021, Table 1 |
| White yam, fried | 59.13 ± 10.32 | Medium | Ampofo 2021, Table 1 |
| Yellow yam (D. cayenensis), fried | 65.08 ± 18.52 | Medium | Ampofo 2021, Table 1 |
| Water yam, fried | 69.16 ± 24.33 | Medium | Ampofo 2021, Table 1 |
| Yellow yam, roasted | 70.62 ± 20.80 | High | Ampofo 2021, Table 1 |
| Yellow yam, boiled | 75.18 ± 22.19 | High | Ampofo 2021, Table 1 |

Authors' conclusion: white yam diets had relatively lower GI than yellow and water yam; boiling generally gave lower GI for white and water yam and could be used to prepare lower-GI meals. Note the large standard deviations and small sample. Frying adds oil and energy (see WAFCT: fried yam has much higher energy per 100 g than boiled yam).
