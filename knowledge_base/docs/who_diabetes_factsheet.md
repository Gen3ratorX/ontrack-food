# Diabetes basics — notes from the WHO fact sheet

- Diabetes is a chronic disease that occurs when the pancreas does not produce enough insulin or when the body cannot effectively use the insulin it produces. Insulin is a hormone that regulates blood glucose.
- Hyperglycaemia (raised blood glucose) is a common effect of uncontrolled diabetes and over time seriously damages many body systems, especially nerves and blood vessels.
- WHO reports that the number of people living with diabetes rose from 200 million in 1990 to 830 million in 2022, with prevalence rising faster in low- and middle-income countries. In 2022, 14% of adults aged 18+ were living with diabetes (7% in 1990), and 59% of adults aged 30+ with diabetes were not taking medication for it; treatment coverage was lowest in low- and middle-income countries.
- WHO reports diabetes was the direct cause of 2.0 million deaths in 2023.
- Symptoms can include feeling very thirsty, needing to urinate more often, blurred vision, feeling tired and losing weight unintentionally. In type 2 diabetes symptoms can be mild and go unnoticed for years.
- Over time diabetes can damage blood vessels in the heart, eyes, kidneys and nerves, raising the risk of heart attack, stroke, kidney failure, vision loss and foot ulcers that may lead to amputation.
- Type 1 diabetes is characterised by deficient insulin production and requires daily insulin. Its cause and prevention are not known.
- Type 2 diabetes affects how the body uses glucose; contributing factors include overweight, too little exercise and genetics. It is often preventable, and early diagnosis matters.
- A healthy diet, regular physical activity, keeping a healthy body weight and avoiding tobacco can prevent or delay type 2 diabetes. Diabetes can be treated with diet, physical activity and medication, and its consequences avoided through regular screening for complications and timely treatment.
