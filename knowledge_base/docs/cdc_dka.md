# Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) — CDC

DKA is a serious complication of diabetes that can be life-threatening. It is most common in type 1 diabetes, but people with type 2 diabetes can also develop it. It happens when the body does not have enough insulin to let blood sugar into cells for energy; the liver breaks down fat, producing acids called ketones that can build up to dangerous levels.

## Signs and symptoms
Early: being very thirsty; urinating much more than usual.
If untreated, more severe symptoms can appear quickly: fast, deep breathing; dry skin and mouth; flushed face; fruity-smelling breath; headache; muscle stiffness or aches; being very tired; nausea and vomiting; stomach pain. Sometimes DKA is the first sign of diabetes in people not yet diagnosed.

## Causes
Very high blood sugar and low insulin. The two most common causes are illness and missed insulin doses, a clogged insulin pump, or the wrong insulin dose. Others: heart attack or stroke, injury, alcohol or drug use, and certain medicines such as some diuretics and corticosteroids.

## Prevention
Check blood sugar often, especially when sick; keep blood sugar in target range; take medicines as prescribed even if you feel fine; talk to your doctor about adjusting insulin for food, activity or sickness.

## Testing and when to get emergency help
If you are sick or your blood sugar is 250 mg/dL (about 13.9 mmol/L) or above, check blood sugar every 4 to 6 hours and check urine for ketones. Elevated ketones are a sign of DKA, which is a medical emergency needing immediate treatment.
Go to the emergency room or call emergency services right away (CDC says 911; in Ghana call 112 or 193) if:
- blood sugar stays at 300 mg/dL (about 16.7 mmol/L) or above
- breath smells fruity
- you are vomiting and cannot keep food or drinks down
- you are having trouble breathing
- you have multiple signs and symptoms of DKA

## Treatment
DKA is treated in hospital: fluid replacement, electrolyte replacement, insulin, and treatment of the underlying illness (for example antibiotics for infection).
