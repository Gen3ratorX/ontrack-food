# ADA Standards of Care 2026 — nutrition and activity (paraphrased)

- No single eating pattern fits everyone with diabetes; meal plans should be individualised and built with the care team, ideally with a registered dietitian (Rec 5.10, 5.13). ADA encourages thinking in foods and eating patterns rather than single nutrients.
- Culturally appropriate diabetes self-management education and support (DSMES) is recommended for everyone with diabetes (Rec 5.1, 5.5).
- Eating patterns should emphasise non-starchy vegetables, whole fruit, legumes, lean proteins, whole grains, nuts and seeds, and low-fat dairy or alternatives, and minimise red meat, sugar-sweetened beverages, sweets, refined grains and processed/ultra-processed foods (Rec 5.14).
- Reducing carbohydrate intake may improve glycaemia for some adults; limiting processed foods is one way (Rec 5.15).
- Supplements such as cinnamon, aloe vera, chromium or magnesium are not recommended for glucose benefit (Rec 5.16).
- Alcohol: do not exceed recommended limits; non-drinkers should not start. Alcohol can cause delayed hypoglycaemia, especially with insulin or insulin secretagogues, so glucose monitoring after drinking matters (Rec 5.18–5.19).
- Sodium (salt): limit sodium to less than 2,300 mg/day as clinically appropriate (Rec 5.20).
- Prefer water over other beverages; replace sugar-sweetened beverages, including juices, with water or low/no-calorie drinks (Rec 5.21, 5.25).
- Choose minimally processed, high-fibre carbohydrate sources — at least 14 g fibre per 1,000 kcal (Rec 5.24).
- People on fixed insulin doses benefit from consistent carbohydrate timing and amount — the insulin plan itself is set by the clinician (Rec 5.28).
- More plant protein (nuts, seeds, legumes) and limiting saturated fat to lower cardiovascular risk (Rec 5.29, 5.31).
- Religious fasting (for example Ramadan) needs a pre-fasting risk assessment and treatment review with the care team well in advance (Rec 5.32–5.33).
- Physical activity for most adults: 150 minutes or more per week of moderate-to-vigorous aerobic activity over at least 3 days, with no more than 2 consecutive days without activity, plus 2–3 resistance sessions per week; interrupt prolonged sitting at least every 30 minutes (Rec 5.34, 5.36, 5.37).
