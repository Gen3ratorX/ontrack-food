# Managing sick days — CDC

## Prepare before getting sick
Keep insulin, other diabetes medicines and easy-to-make foods for several weeks. If you cannot eat meals, CDC advises eating or drinking about 50 grams of carbohydrate every 4 hours.

## If you get sick
Blood sugar can be hard to manage when sick, because illness hormones can raise blood sugar and you may eat or drink less. If you take insulin, being sick may affect how much you need — your doctor decides this. CDC tips even if blood sugar is in range:
- Continue taking insulin and diabetes pills as usual (unless your doctor tells you otherwise).
- Test blood sugar every 4 hours and keep track.
- Drink plenty of water to prevent dehydration.
- Try to eat as you normally would.
- Weigh yourself every day; losing weight without trying is a sign of high blood sugar.
- Check your temperature morning and evening.
If ketones are present in urine, call your doctor right away.

## When to go to the emergency room (CDC)
- trouble breathing
- ketones in your urine
- cannot keep liquids down for more than 4 hours, or food for more than 24 hours
- losing 5 pounds or more during the illness
- blood sugar lower than 60 mg/dL (3.3 mmol/L)
- vomiting and/or severe diarrhoea for more than 6 hours
- temperature over 101 °F (about 38.3 °C) for 24 hours
If needed, have someone else call your doctor or take you to hospital, to avoid unsafe driving.
