# Foods with no verified GI value in this knowledge base

OnTrack must NOT state a GI number for these foods. Say that a measured value was not found, and fall back to general guidance (portion size, plate method, fibre, preparation method). If asked for the GI of waakye, jollof, or gari, say the evidence is unavailable. Do not invent a number.

- Waakye (rice and beans): no directly measured GI for a typical Ghanaian waakye meal was found. Do not average the GI of rice and beans.
- Jollof rice: no Ghanaian measured GI found. It is a white-rice dish; refined grains raise blood sugar faster (CDC).
- Plantain (boiled, roasted/kofi brokeman, fried/kelewele, ampesi): a 1997 Ghanaian study (PubMed 9329286) compared unripe plantain, Ga kenkey, gari, rice and yam in mixed meals in 10 healthy men; Ga kenkey gave the smallest glucose changes and yam the least favourable; the abstract gives no numeric GI values. Unripe (green) plantain is higher in fibre than ripe plantain per WAFCT 2019 (see wafct2019_ghana_foods).
- Gari (eba, gari soakings): studied in the 1997 mixed-meal paper, no numeric GI in the accessible abstract.
- Kontomire (cocoyam leaves), garden eggs, okra: non-starchy vegetables with low available carbohydrate per WAFCT 2019 — GI is not a meaningful measure for them.
- Hausa koko, rice water, tom brown, sobolo with sugar, malt drinks: no GI found; sweetened drinks and porridges with added sugar should be limited (ADA, CDC).
- A 2024 Ghanaian study of fufu types reportedly gave GI values for plantain fufu; the primary paper was not verified for this release, so values are omitted.
