# Treating low blood sugar — CDC

## The 15-15 rule
If your blood sugar is low (below 70 mg/dL, about 3.9 mmol/L):
1. Have 15 grams of carbs, then wait 15 minutes.
2. Check your blood sugar again. If it is still less than 70 mg/dL, repeat.
3. Keep repeating until your blood sugar is back in your target range.
4. After treating the low, eat a balanced snack or meal with protein and carbs.

Young children may need less than 15 grams of carbs; ask a doctor how much a child needs. Check blood sugar often when lows are more likely, such as in hot weather or when travelling.

## Foods to treat low blood sugar
Sugars need to be absorbed quickly. Foods high in fibre (fruit, beans, lentils) or fat (chocolate, baked goods) slow absorption, so they are poor first choices. CDC examples of about 15 g fast-acting carbohydrate:
- 4 ounces (half a cup) of juice or regular (non-diet) soda
- 1 tablespoon of sugar, honey or syrup
- hard candies, jellybeans or gumdrops (check label)
- 3–4 glucose tablets (follow instructions)
- 1 dose of glucose gel (usually 1 tube)

## Severely low blood sugar
If blood sugar drops below 55 mg/dL it is considered severely low and the 15-15 rule may not be enough; the person may not be able to check or treat it themselves. Injectable glucagon (prescription) is the best treatment for severe lows. Contact a doctor for emergency medical treatment immediately after a glucagon injection. If the person is awake and able to swallow, give a fast-acting sugar source and then a longer-acting carbohydrate with protein.

## Prevention
Monitor frequently. If lows keep happening, share your blood sugar records, medicines, activity and food patterns with your doctor. Do not make any changes to your medicines without talking to your doctor.

## OnTrack safety note (project-authored)
A person who is unconscious, having a seizure, or cannot swallow must NOT be given food or drink by mouth. Call emergency services (Ghana: 112, or the National Ambulance Service on 193).
