# Healthy diet — notes from the WHO fact sheet

- Healthy diets protect against malnutrition and noncommunicable diseases including diabetes, heart disease, stroke and cancer.
- WHO's four principles: adequacy, balance, moderation and diversity. A variety of minimally processed and unprocessed foods low in unhealthy fats, free sugars and sodium is the foundation of a healthy diet. The exact make-up varies with the person, cultural context, locally available foods and dietary customs.
- Carbohydrates: for most people a variety of unrefined carbohydrates should make up a significant portion of the diet (WHO gives approximately 45–75% of total daily energy), coming mainly from whole grains, vegetables, fruits and pulses. WHO examples of whole grains include unprocessed maize, millet, oats, wheat and brown rice; pulses include lentils, chickpeas, beans and dried peas.
- Most fruit juices, including those without added sugar, contain significant free sugars, which should be limited.
- Everyone older than 10 should aim for at least 400 g of fruits and vegetables per day.
- Note for OnTrack: WHO general-population guidance is not diabetes-specific; individual carbohydrate targets for people with diabetes should be set with a clinician or dietitian (see ADA notes).
