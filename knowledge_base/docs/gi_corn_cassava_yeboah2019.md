# GI of Ghanaian corn and cassava staples — Yeboah et al. 2019

Design: crossover trial, 10 healthy adults (5 men, 5 women); 50 g available carbohydrate portions vs 50 g glucose reference. Akple, kafa and kokonte were eaten with groundnut soup with beef, abolo with anchovies; the authors measured the accompaniments separately and subtracted their GI to report values "without accompaniments" (an unusual method — treat the numbers with caution).

| Food (as reported, without accompaniments) | GI | Class | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kokonte, locally made (cassava) | 7 | Low | Yeboah 2019 |
| Kokonte, processed flour (cassava) | 18 | Low | Yeboah 2019 |
| Kafa (corn) | 29 | Low | Yeboah 2019 |
| Abolo (corn) | 58 | Medium | Yeboah 2019 |
| Akple (corn) | 69 | Medium | Yeboah 2019 |

The difference between local and processed kokonte was not significant (p > 0.05).

Caveats: very small sample of healthy volunteers; the subtraction method and the unusually low kokonte values need confirmation; a separate study (Ghana, 2020, predicted in-vitro GI) reported a predicted GI of about 40 for kokonte from sun-dried flour and about 61 from solar-dried flour — so kokonte GI is uncertain and depends on processing. Do not present kokonte as a food that "does not raise blood sugar".
