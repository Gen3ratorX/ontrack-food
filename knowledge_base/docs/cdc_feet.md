# Foot care — CDC

About half of people with diabetes have some nerve damage, most often in the feet and legs, which can cause loss of feeling. Without pain you may not notice a cut, blister or sore. Nerve damage and poor blood flow raise the risk of foot ulcers that can get infected and, if infection does not improve, lead to amputation.

Risk factors: blood sugar that is hard to manage, long duration of diabetes, overweight, age over 40, high blood pressure, high cholesterol.

Daily habits: check feet every day for cuts, redness, swelling, sores, blisters, corns, calluses or nail changes (use a mirror or ask family). Wash daily in warm (not hot) water, do not soak, dry completely, use lotion on top and bottom but not between toes. Never go barefoot, even inside. Wear well-fitting shoes with socks; check shoes for pebbles. Trim nails straight across. Do not remove corns or calluses yourself or with over-the-counter products. Get feet checked at every visit and have a full foot exam at least yearly. Don't smoke; be physically active (10–20 minutes a day is better than an hour once a week).

See a doctor right away (do not wait for the next appointment) for: leg pain or cramping during activity; tingling, burning or pain in the feet; loss of feeling for touch, heat or cold; change in foot shape; dry cracked skin; change in colour or temperature of the feet; thickened yellow toenails; fungal infections; a blister, sore, ulcer, infected corn or ingrown toenail.
