# Ghanaian food glossary (descriptions; no nutrient figures)

- Banku: fermented corn dough, often mixed with cassava dough, cooked and stirred into a smooth stiff ball; eaten with okro stew, pepper and grilled tilapia, or groundnut/light soup.
- Kenkey (Ga kenkey, Fante kenkey/dokono): fermented maize dough partly cooked, wrapped in corn husks or plantain leaves and boiled; eaten with fried fish, pepper (shito or fresh pepper) and onions.
- Fufu: pounded boiled cassava with plantain or cocoyam (or yam), eaten with light soup, palm-nut soup or groundnut soup. Also made from packaged fufu flour.
- Tuo zaafi (TZ): stiff maize (or millet) porridge from northern Ghana, eaten with green leaf (ayoyo) soup.
- Akple: Ewe corn-flour (sometimes with cassava) stiff porridge eaten with okro soup. Abolo: steamed fermented corn/rice dough cake. Kafa: corn-based Ewe staple.
- Kokonte (face the wall): dried cassava flour cooked into a brown stiff paste, eaten with groundnut or palm-nut soup.
- Gari: grated, fermented, roasted cassava granules; eaten as eba, with beans (gari and beans / red red), or soaked in water with sugar and milk ("gari soakings").
- Waakye: rice cooked with cowpeas/beans and sorghum leaves, served with stew, gari, spaghetti (talia), egg, fish or meat.
- Jollof rice: rice cooked in tomato, onion and pepper stew with oil and spices.
- Red red: beans stew cooked in palm oil, served with fried ripe plantain.
- Plantain: eaten boiled (ampesi), roasted (kofi brokeman), fried (kelewele, spiced), or pounded into fufu. Unripe (green) plantain is starchier and less sweet than ripe plantain.
- Ampesi: boiled yam, plantain, cocoyam or cassava eaten with kontomire stew or garden egg stew.
- Kontomire: cocoyam leaves, cooked into kontomire stew ("palava sauce") with palm oil, egusi, fish or eggs.
- Garden eggs: small local eggplants used in stews and eaten with ampesi.
- Okro (okra): used in okro stew/soup.
- Hausa koko: spicy fermented millet porridge often sweetened with sugar, eaten with koose (bean fritters).
- Tom brown: roasted corn (and sometimes groundnut/soya) porridge. Rice water: rice porridge usually with sugar and milk.
- Sobolo: hibiscus (roselle) drink often sweetened with sugar. Asaana: caramelised fermented corn drink.
- Shito: hot pepper sauce cooked in oil with dried fish/shrimp.
- Light soup: tomato and pepper based soup with fish, chicken or goat. Palm-nut soup (abenkwan) and groundnut soup (nkate nkwan) are richer in fat.
